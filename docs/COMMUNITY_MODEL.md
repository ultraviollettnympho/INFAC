# INFAC COMMUNITY MODEL

INFAC has three social layers. Keeping them separate protects both the collective and the people merely passing through it.

## Collective

The collective is the committed body.

It may make decisions, produce shared work, maintain resources, organize events, publish material, and accept responsibilities on behalf of INFAC.

Participation should be explicit rather than inferred from appearance in the archive.

## Commons

The commons is the practical layer.

It includes knowledge, resource directories, mutual-aid tools, harm-reduction information, housing leads, food access, care pathways, shared tools, and other things that become more valuable when more people can use them.

The commons should not require ideological agreement or collective membership to be useful.

## Network

The network is the field of correspondence around INFAC.

An artist, venue, organizer, publication, place, or project can be connected without being an INFAC member.

Relationships should be described accurately: `worked with`, `hosted`, `published`, `in correspondence`, `credited to`, `shared a venue with`, and so on are different claims.

## Affinity and future formations

INFAC may eventually develop more intimate affinity groups, artist syndicates, worker/resource unions, or temporary project cells.

Those structures should be documented as distinct formations rather than forcing every relationship into one membership hierarchy.

## Data implications

The future data model should be capable of representing:

- affiliation status
- relationship type
- consent / publication status
- contributor role
- stewardship responsibility
- visibility level
- provenance

The absence of a field is not permission to infer it.
