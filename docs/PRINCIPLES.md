# INFAC PRINCIPLES

## 1. Make the useful thing usable

If someone needs a phone number, address, service, meeting time, or warning, give it to them plainly.

## 2. Weirdness is an expressive layer, not an access barrier

Occult, ritual, symbolic, poetic, and experimental interface language can deepen the experience. It cannot be the password for ordinary functionality.

## 3. Preserve unfinishedness

A fragment can be an honest record. A project can be dormant. An event can be lost. An argument can remain unresolved.

## 4. Correspondence over hierarchy

Favor relationships, adjacency, and lateral discovery over a rigid hierarchy of “important” and “unimportant” culture.

## 5. Commons over extraction

Whenever possible, infrastructure should increase collective capacity rather than harvest attention, data, labor, or money from people with the least of it.

## 6. Consent and provenance matter

Do not publish people's work, identities, locations, or stories merely because the database can hold them.

## 7. Protect precarious places

Not every useful location should be mapped publicly. Publicity can destroy the thing it claims to document.

## 8. Anti-surveillance by default

No tracking stack is required to make an archive useful. Data collection must have a specific, defensible purpose.

## 9. Portability

The content model should not become inseparable from one hosting provider, AI provider, map provider, or social platform.

## 10. Build for contribution without demanding performance

INFAC should eventually accept contributions from people who have little time, unstable access, limited money, or no interest in becoming influencers.
