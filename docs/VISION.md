# INFAC VISION

INFAC exists because the official structures around art, survival, housing, drugs, work, publishing, and community repeatedly fail the people who need them most.

We are not trying to build a nicer version of those structures.

We are building another kind of infrastructure.

INFAC is a creative collective, an underground press, a commons, an archive, a resource instrument, and a loose network of people and places in correspondence. These functions overlap, but they are not interchangeable.

## The proposition

Art does not need to justify its existence by becoming a product.

Care does not need to become charity theater.

A resource directory does not need to become a surveillance system.

An archive does not need to pretend everything was finished.

A network does not need to become a brand-owned membership platform.

The interface should make those propositions tangible.

## What INFAC should become

A person should eventually be able to enter INFAC from several directions:

- looking for something made
- looking for someone to make something with
- looking for a place or event
- looking for food, housing, care, harm reduction, or practical help
- looking for a zine or argument
- looking for the history of a project
- looking for a way to contribute
- simply wandering through the archive

None of those entrances should require understanding the mythology first.

## The LA condition

The move to Los Angeles creates an unusually useful field condition for INFAC: new neighborhoods, new artists, new survival problems, new resources, new places, and new correspondences.

INFAC should document that reality without turning people or precarious places into content commodities.

The site should become useful as a local instrument while retaining portability to other cities and contexts.

## Long horizon

The long-term possibility is not merely a larger website.

It is a distributed cultural infrastructure where people can make, publish, find one another, exchange resources, survive bad conditions, and create reasons to work together that do not depend entirely on market validation.

The software should remain subordinate to that purpose.
